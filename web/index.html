01 · Problem

Problem

Ingesting external merchant offers and marketplace listings at retail scale introduces four recurring failure modes that corrupt Buy Box pricing, search filters, and customer trust.

Selection & Catalog Quality

Overview

CatalogDawn unifies external merchant offer ingestion and closed-loop catalog normalization on serverless AWS infrastructure. Incoming HTML, JSON-LD, and CSV feeds pass a GS1 Modulo-10 barcode check and unit-price outlier guard in 0.048 ms median before entering entity resolution. Blocking keys and 64-permutation character-shingle MinHash score candidate ASINs in 1.128 ms median while hard vetoes on pack count, storage capacity, voltage, color, and size hold variant mixups at 0.0% across 200 benchmark pairs (where a text-similarity baseline conflates 96.2% of variant traps). Multi-marketplace claims across US, DE, and JP canonicalize to standard units in 0.05 ms and cascade ambiguous prose or seller contradictions to Amazon Bedrock (us.amazon.nova-micro-v1:0 and us.amazon.nova-lite-v1:0), while a return-defect self-learning loop validates candidate rules on a 64-record labeled holdout set before promotion.

01 · BARCODE & PRICE HIJACKS

Accessories Claim High-Value Product Barcodes

External feeds frequently attach a laptop or 6-pack filter UPC to a $6.99 protective sleeve or loose single unit, or ship corrupted check digits that poison downstream catalog joins.

02 · VARIANT CONFLATION

High Title Overlap Merges 1-Packs Into Multi-Packs

A 1-pack charger and a 4-pack charger share over 94% of their title tokens. Pure embedding or Jaccard similarity merges them onto the same ASIN, breaking unit pricing and fulfillment.

03 · LOCALE & SELLER CONFLICTS

Heterogeneous Units and Contradictory Claims

Listings arrive as 16.9 fl oz in US, 0,065 kW in DE, and 65ワット in JP, while third-party sellers mark items dishwasher safe despite OEM manuals stating hand wash only.

04 · STATIC TAXONOMY RULES

Customer Return Defects Repeat Without Feedback

Buyer return logs reveal hidden spec defects such as 100V single-voltage imports sold as dual-voltage or M10 adapters missing thread pitch, yet static rulebooks fail to adapt automatically.

03 · Implementation

Implementation

Four modular Python 3.12 subsystems handle external merchant feed parsing, variant-safe ASIN matching, cross-marketplace attribute reconciliation, and return-signal rule synthesis.

12 / 12 Unit Tests Passing
01 · RAW MERCHANT OFFER EXTRACTOR & GTIN GUARD catalog_dawn/offer_extractor.py
External merchant feeds arrive as messy HTML pages with embedded JSON-LD, JSON payloads, or CSV spreadsheets. CatalogDawn extracts commercial offer fields, verifies barcode check digits, converts foreign currencies, and quarantines unit-price anomalies before any offer reaches the catalog.
extract_merchant_offer() parses HTML DOM and application/ld+json graphs in 0.048 ms median (0.135 ms p95), validates 8/12/13/14-digit GS1 Modulo-10 check digits via validate_gtin_check_digit(), normalizes EUR, GBP, and JPY landed prices, and blocks accessory UPC hijacks (< 22% or > 3.8x reference ASIN median) across 48 / 48 benchmark payloads.
02 · VARIANT-SAFE ASIN ENTITY MATCHER catalog_dawn/entity_resolver.py
Matching an external offer to an existing Amazon ASIN requires separating genuine lexical paraphrases from sibling variants. A 1-pack offer and a 6-pack offer share nearly identical titles, so CatalogDawn blocks any merge whenever pack count, storage, voltage, color, or size disagree.
VariantSafeEntityResolver.resolve_offer() narrows candidates via brand:product_type blocking keys, computes 64-permutation CRC32 character 3-gram MinHash + token Jaccard similarity in 1.128 ms median, enforces evaluate_variant_vetoes() across five protected variant axes (0.0% conflation across 104 adversarial traps vs 96.2% naive conflation), and routes unseen valid bundles to CREATE_CHILD_VARIANT_ASIN.
03 · CROSS-LOCALE NORMALIZER & CONTRADICTION RESOLVER catalog_dawn/ontology_normalizer.py
Product attributes across US, German, and Japanese marketplaces use incompatible units and often carry conflicting claims between third-party sellers and manufacturer manuals. CatalogDawn standardizes units, resolves conflicts by source authority, and blocks ungrounded LLM guesses.
canonicalize_attribute_value() converts 0,065 kW and 65ワット to 65 W, 16.9 fl oz to 500 ml, and 20000 mAh @ 3.7V to 74.0 Wh in 0.05 ms. Authority weights rank OEM_MANUAL (1.00) and VERIFIED_RETURN_CLUSTER (0.92) above SELLER_STRUCTURED_ATTR (0.50), cascading ambiguous records to us.amazon.nova-micro-v1:0 (348.0 ms p50) and us.amazon.nova-lite-v1:0 (634.0 ms p50) with span verification.
04 · RETURN-SIGNAL SELF-LEARNING LOOP & AUDIT STORE catalog_dawn/self_learning_loop.py · audit_store.py
When buyers return items because a listing promised dual voltage on a 100V unit or omitted M10 thread pitch, CatalogDawn clusters those return reasons, asks Amazon Bedrock to propose a validation rule, and tests the rule against a labeled holdout catalog before enabling it.
synthesize_and_validate_rule() prompts us.amazon.nova-lite-v1:0 with clustered return defects and gates promotion via evaluate_rule_on_holdout() across 64 labeled listings (requiring >= 95% precision and <= 2% false-positive rate). Three high-precision rules promoted automatically (lifting holdout defect recall from 37.5% to 93.8%), one over-broad shipping-dent rule was rejected (83.3% FPR), and every mutation is hash-chained in SQLite WAL.
04 · Trade-Offs

Trade-Offs

Every architectural decision in CatalogDawn balances precision vs. recall, inference cost vs. latency, and autonomous rule promotion vs. catalog safety.

Design Decisions
01 · HARD VARIANT VETOES VS SOFT EMBEDDINGS

Zero Pack-Size Mixups Over Aggressive Auto-Merging

Enforcing strict vetoes on pack_count, storage_gb, voltage_v, color, and size means an offer missing explicit pack-count proof routes to child-ASIN creation or review rather than auto-merging. In return, false variant conflation drops from 96.2% (naive text baseline) to 0.0%, preventing single-unit offers from hijacking multi-pack Buy Boxes.

02 · 2-TIER BEDROCK CASCADE VS SINGLE FRONTIER MODEL

79.0% Lower Inference Cost per 10,000 SKUs

Running deterministic ontology rules first (0.05 ms), escalating unstructured prose extraction to us.amazon.nova-micro-v1:0 (348.0 ms), and reserving us.amazon.nova-lite-v1:0 (634.0 ms) for multi-seller contradictions adds routing logic, but cuts Bedrock cost per 10,000 SKUs from $0.5580 to $0.1171 while preserving 100.0% canonical accuracy.

03 · HOLDOUT PROMOTION GATE VS INSTANT RULE DEPLOY

Blocking Over-Broad AI Rules Before Catalog Enforcement

Requiring every AI-proposed validation rule to score >= 95% precision and <= 2% false-positive rate on a labeled holdout catalog delays noisy rules such as carrier box-dent keyword filters (caught at 83.3% FPR and rejected). That guardrail keeps clean holdout false quarantines at 0 / 48 while lifting defect catch recall to 93.8%.

04 · SERVERLESS SQLITE WAL VS PROVISIONED CLUSTERS

$0.00/Hour Idle Footprint vs Ephemeral Container Storage

Deploying on CloudFront + private S3 OAC + Python 3.12 Lambda with SQLite WAL in /tmp eliminates always-on EC2, RDS, and OpenSearch hourly charges, with the trade-off that transient demo mutations reset when warm Lambda containers recycle. At multi-billion-ASIN production scale, the blocking and rule tables partition cleanly onto DynamoDB and S3 Iceberg.
